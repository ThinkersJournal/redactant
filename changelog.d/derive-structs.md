kind: `#[derive(Redact)]` for structs (feature `derive`; new crate `redactant-derive`)
---
Adds `redactant-derive` (a proc-macro crate carrying this crate's version, re-exported as `redactant::Redact` under the new off-by-default feature `derive`) and `#[derive(Redact)]` for structs (named, tuple, unit; generics with the bounds the attributes imply). Every field must state what it may reveal: `#[redact(keep)]` (needs `Keep`), `#[redact(fingerprint)]` (needs `AsRef<[u8]>`), `#[redact]` (withhold) or `#[redact(nested)]` (needs `Redact`); a `Secret<..>` field needs no attribute and cannot be `keep`; an unannotated field is a compile error naming the field and the choices, unless the struct says `#[redact(default = "redact")]` or `"fingerprint"` (`"keep"` is refused). `skip`, conflicting options, unions and enums (a later release) are errors. `Redact` gains a provided `schema()` returning a canonical string of a type's field dispositions (the derive writes it; Option/Vec/array forward to their element), so a consumer can hash it to notice a changed disposition (AuditAnt's answer 6). Additive only. Verified by `tests/derive.rs` (D1-D5 through the macro, schema, defaults, generics, hygiene with shadowed `Option`/`Some`/`Vec`/`String`) and nine compile-fail cases each with a passing twin (`tests/ui-derive*`); CI now runs the workspace and the derive compile-fail target. Also records the PM's read of the patent re-sweep 3 (document, not the patents). The PM publishes `redactant-derive` before `redactant`.
